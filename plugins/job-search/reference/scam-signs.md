# Scam signs

Used by the scam-check, sweep, fit-check, recruiter-reply and apply skills, before anyone clicks a link or replies.

Job scams are common, and **task scams** are now among the most reported kinds: someone offers pay to "rate", "like", "review" or "optimize" apps, products, hotels or videos, shows small early earnings, then asks for a deposit (often in crypto) to "unlock" more tasks or withdraw what was "earned". Nobody legitimate pays you to rate things and then asks you to pay in.

## Check each sign (yes or no, quote the line)

**Task scam signs**
1. Pay for liking, rating, reviewing, "optimizing" or "boosting" apps, products, videos, hotels or orders.
2. A "task", "order", "set" or "level" system, with commissions or a balance on a website or app.
3. Any deposit, top-up, "reset fee" or "unlock fee", or a balance that goes negative and must be paid back.
4. Crypto (USDT, Bitcoin, any wallet) for pay or deposits.
5. A first contact out of nowhere by text, WhatsApp, Telegram or Signal, or a move from email to one of them.

**General job scam signs**
6. Money, a check to deposit, gift cards, or bank details asked before a real offer.
7. Buying equipment or software from "their vendor".
8. Interview only by chat or text, or an offer with no interview at all.
9. Pay far above normal for simple work, or "no experience needed, earn $X a day".
10. A company that uses a free email address (gmail, outlook, yahoo, proton) for hiring, or a sender domain that does not match the company's real website.
11. A sender whose name, company, title or role changes, or odd spelling and mixed alphabets in names or links.
12. Pressure to act today, or secrecy ("don't tell anyone about this job").
13. A link, shortened URL or attachment to open before anything is confirmed.
14. Early asks for her home address, ID, Social Security number, date of birth or bank login.
15. A job she never applied for that "already approved" her.

## Verdict

- **LIKELY SCAM**: any of signs 1 to 4, or sign 6, 7 or 14, or three or more other signs.
- **CHECK FIRST**: one or two other signs. Common for real but pushy agency recruiters ("let me know today"), so do not call it a scam on that alone.
- **LOOKS REAL**: no signs.

Give the two strongest reasons, quoted.

## What to tell her

For LIKELY SCAM or CHECK FIRST, at most 3 steps:
1. Do not click links, open files, send details or pay anything.
2. Find the company's real website by searching its name yourself, and contact them through it to check.
3. Report the message on the platform it came through. In the US: reportfraud.ftc.gov; if money was lost, also ic3.gov.

If she already clicked, sent details or paid: tell her to change any password she typed, call her bank if money or bank details went out, and report it as above. Calm, plain words.

For LOOKS REAL: "This looks like a real message. The fit check is next."
